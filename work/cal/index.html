02 / COMMUNITY ACTION LAB

Turning neighborhood concerns into an action plan

A resident-and-city partner team shaped a draft proposal for cleaner, safer shared spaces in Ellwood Park and McElderry Park.

Community Action LabTeam proposal$5,000 draft budget
02

Starting with what residents named

As part of the Southeast District Community Action Lab cohort, I worked with a resident-and-city partner team on a draft neighborhood improvement proposal for Ellwood Park and McElderry Park. Residents raised concerns including persistent trash, rodents, open-air drug activity, and damage to street trees and shared spaces.

The team focused on practical ways to connect visible neighborhood improvements with resident participation, local partnerships, and follow-through.

Ideas the team explored

Resident outreach

Bilingual door hangers, block-level engagement, cleanup activities, and a neighborhood challenge designed to invite participation.

Greener, cared-for spaces

Concepts included house-front planters, mulch and low fencing around trees, and “plants for trash” as a positive neighborhood message.

Shared-space safety

The team discussed ideas such as alley gates and improvements that could help residents feel safer using neighborhood spaces.

Learning as we go

The draft implementation plan proposed collaboration, flyer distribution, a launch date, and later data collection and evaluation.

My contribution

I coordinated proposal development, the draft budget, and evaluation planning. I also helped shape the outreach approach, implementation plan, and presentation so the team could explain the neighborhood need and its proposed response.

The draft project budget was $5,000: $4,050 for staffing and $950 for materials, with PEP listed as a partner or sponsor. The presentation also requested support from the Mayor’s Office, BCHD, behavioral health specialists, and an additional $35,000 to support a year of operation.

Status: This was a Community Action Lab team proposal. It was not funded or implemented; the ideas and budget described here are planning work, not completed outcomes.

Project materials

The team prepared a poster summarizing its draft goals, roles, timeline, budget, and proposed request. The original poster included individual names and a resident quote, so it is not reproduced in this public portfolio.

Team roles

Each team member brought a distinct focus: project architecture and the Ellwood Park perspective; outreach and resource navigation; McElderry Park relationships and potential partners; participant engagement and feedback; and safety and city processes.
